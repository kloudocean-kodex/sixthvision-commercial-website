What the photographs have to prove

Clear-span, docks, hardstand, and the yard a truck can use.

Industrial property photography fails when it only makes an empty hall look large. Leasing agents need the loading face, the internal clearance, the racking if the building is occupied, the roller doors, and the hardstand where vehicles turn. Office and amenities matter when they are part of the tenancy, photographed so they read as part of the same building rather than a separate fit-out.

Factories and working warehouses are shot as they operate, with the occupier’s agreement. Vacant stock is shot for scale and light, without dressing the building as something it is not.

Direct answers

Industrial photography questions.

Do you photograph occupied warehouses?

Yes, with the occupier’s access and any induction the site requires. Working racking, docks and plant are photographed as they are.

Can cold-store and logistics yards be included?

Yes. Dock faces, refrigerated vehicles and the distribution interior are a normal part of an industrial brief, subject to site rules.

Which parts of Melbourne?

The West, North and South-East industrial corridors, inner industrial such as Maidstone and Williamstown, and Geelong. CBD agents often brief buildings that sit in those corridors.

Will you add a drone and a floor plan?

Yes. Aerial stills, aerial film, a measured site plan and a tenancy floor plan can be scoped with the ground photography so the agent is not coordinating three suppliers.

Is the mapping a survey?

No. Dimensions on an aerial or a marketing floor plan are for the campaign. Legal survey and certification stay with the appropriate professional.
