Drone, not just a pretty aerial

The word agents type is drone. The job is still the property.

Searchers ask for commercial drone photography. On a campaign the same work is often called aerial. Sixth Vision treats them as one service: a CASA-certified aircraft, a frame that shows the holding, and — when the brief needs it — measurements drawn onto that frame.

A drone still can show the roof, the hardstand, the dock face, the frontage and the roads that feed the site. Drone video can carry a short approach, useful in a listing film or a developer update. Neither replaces a ground photograph of the interior. They answer the questions the ground camera cannot.

Direct answers

Commercial drone photography questions.

Are you CASA-certified?

Yes. Aerial work is flown as CASA-certified drone photography. The flight is planned to the site, the airspace and the campaign, not as a generic flyover.

Do you shoot drone stills and video?

Both. Stills for the listing and the brochure, and aerial film when the campaign needs an approach or a site context sequence. Say which one the enquiry has to see.

Can you put measurements on the aerial?

Yes, where the brief includes it. Boundary, area, frontage and amenity can be drawn onto the drone frame. That mapping is for the campaign. It does not replace a survey.

Is a drone flight a legal survey?

No. A measured aerial helps an agent explain the site. Title, boundary certification and statutory area certificates stay with the qualified survey.

What if the weather will not fly?

Ground photography can still be shot. The drone is booked for the next window that airspace and weather allow, against the campaign deadline.
